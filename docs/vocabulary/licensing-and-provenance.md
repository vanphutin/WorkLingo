# Licensing and provenance

The initial implementation was blocked pending permission. Later on 2026-10-22,
the user supplied and reaffirmed a received complimentary licensing email image
for the five A1–C1 books, then supplied their own TXT transcription. The isolated
TXT import records that user-attested evidence and exact scope; it does not claim
independent mailbox authentication. The image, grant and extracted content stay
outside Git. See authorized-txt-import-2026-10-22.md for the measured execution.

The grant is scoped to those five books within WorkLingo: extraction, storage,
adaptation, translation and commercial educational use; form, definitions and
pronunciations. Separate redistribution of raw data/PDFs is not approved.
Attribution: “Vocabulary content from LanGeek (https://langeek.co)”. The imported
TXT source identities are bound to its SHA-256 and keep the evidence reference.
Other sources remain subject to their own approval; free access is not approval.

LanGeek's [copyright guidelines](https://help.langeek.co/legal/copyright-guidelines/)
were inspected on 2026-10-22. The page restricts reproduction/modification/public
display without explicit permission and also describes educational uses. This
does not establish permission for the proposed product storage/distribution.
No legal entitlement is inferred from those general statements.

Approval requires an opaque permission reference, EXTRACT/STORE scope and FORM
permission. DEFINITION and PRONUNCIATION are separately scoped content fields.
Record modification, translation, redistribution and commercial permissions only
when the actual authorization covers them. Confidential documents stay outside
source records, logs and screenshots. Recheck current authorization at each
content stage and read; revocation prevents further use. Retiring an import
must remain possible after revocation.

An alternative to evaluate is Princeton WordNet, whose
[license](https://wordnet.princeton.edu/license-and-commercial-use) permits reuse
subject to its notice/disclaimer requirements. It is not a CEFR-labelled wordlist;
CEFR/program associations would need independent reviewed evidence. It is not
automatically imported by this work. Independently authored/licensed CEFR inputs
can use the same source, occurrence, rights and collection adapters.
