[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-social-cohesion]]
expression: social cohesion
meaning_vi: sự gắn kết xã hội
pronunciation: /ˈsəʊʃəl kəʊˈhiːʒən/
collocations: strengthen social cohesion
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Our centre aims to strengthen social cohesion by creating regular opportunities for residents to cooperate.
common_error: Cohesion is uncountable when naming a quality of society.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-voluntary-work]]
expression: voluntary work
meaning_vi: công việc tình nguyện
pronunciation: /ˈvɒləntəri wɜːk/
collocations: take part in voluntary work
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The voluntary work programme will list responsibilities clearly and reimburse agreed travel expenses.
common_error: Work is uncountable; use a voluntary role for one position.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-social-inclusion]]
expression: social inclusion
meaning_vi: sự hòa nhập xã hội
pronunciation: /ˈsəʊʃəl ɪnˈkluːʒən/
collocations: promote social inclusion
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The social inclusion plan offers quiet sessions as well as busy events, rather than assuming everyone enjoys the same environment.
common_error: Inclusion is a noun; inclusive is an adjective.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-income-inequality]]
expression: income inequality
meaning_vi: bất bình đẳng thu nhập
pronunciation: /ˈɪnkʌm ˌɪnɪˈkwɒləti/
collocations: reduce income inequality
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A discussion on income inequality will distinguish differences in household resources from differences in individual effort.
common_error: Inequality describes a gap, not simply low income for everyone.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-community-engagement]]
expression: community engagement
meaning_vi: sự tham gia và gắn bó với cộng đồng
pronunciation: /kəˈmjuːnəti ɪnˈɡeɪdʒmənt/
collocations: encourage community engagement
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: To improve community engagement, residents will help choose the timetable before bookings open.
common_error: Engagement refers to meaningful involvement, not only audience size.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-public-services]]
expression: public services
meaning_vi: dịch vụ công
pronunciation: /ˈpʌblɪk ˈsɜːvɪsɪz/
collocations: access to public services
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The advice desk will explain public services and direct residents to qualified staff instead of making decisions on their behalf.
common_error: Services is plural when naming different organized provisions.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-ageing-population]]
expression: ageing population
meaning_vi: dân số đang già hóa
pronunciation: /ˈeɪdʒɪŋ ˌpɒpjəˈleɪʃən/
collocations: support an ageing population
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Planning for an ageing population will include varied interests rather than describe all older people as having identical needs.
common_error: Use an before ageing population; ageing is the British spelling.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-civic-responsibility]]
expression: civic responsibility
meaning_vi: trách nhiệm công dân
pronunciation: /ˈsɪvɪk rɪˌspɒnsəˈbɪləti/
collocations: a sense of civic responsibility
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A workshop on civic responsibility will explore both residents' contributions and the council's obligations.
common_error: Civic relates to community participation; civil has several other meanings.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-support-networks]]
expression: support networks
meaning_vi: mạng lưới hỗ trợ
pronunciation: /səˈpɔːt ˈnetwɜːks/
collocations: build support networks
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Existing support networks will be invited to explain their work without sharing private details about members.
common_error: Support modifies networks and stays singular here.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-social-mobility]]
expression: social mobility
meaning_vi: khả năng thay đổi vị thế xã hội
pronunciation: /ˈsəʊʃəl məʊˈbɪləti/
collocations: improve social mobility
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The mentoring discussion will examine social mobility while acknowledging barriers that individual advice cannot remove.
common_error: Mobility here concerns opportunities over time, not transport access alone.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-interpersonal-trust]]
expression: interpersonal trust
meaning_vi: sự tin cậy giữa các cá nhân
pronunciation: /ˌɪntəˈpɜːsənəl trʌst/
collocations: build interpersonal trust
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Small groups can build interpersonal trust through reliable shared tasks and clear ways to raise concerns.
common_error: Trust is normally uncountable in this meaning.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-society-community-shared-resources]]
expression: shared resources
meaning_vi: nguồn lực dùng chung
pronunciation: /ʃeəd rɪˈsɔːsɪz/
collocations: manage shared resources
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A published booking policy will govern shared resources such as meeting rooms, tools and storage space.
common_error: Resources is plural when naming several useful assets.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[CONTENT ielts-society-community-proposal]
type: email
text:
<<<
Dear planning group,
We are residents reviewing a plan for a shared community centre. Please compare the priorities below before our next meeting.
Our centre aims to strengthen social cohesion by creating regular opportunities for residents to cooperate.
The voluntary work programme will list responsibilities clearly and reimburse agreed travel expenses.
The social inclusion plan offers quiet sessions as well as busy events, rather than assuming everyone enjoys the same environment.
A discussion on income inequality will distinguish differences in household resources from differences in individual effort.
To improve community engagement, residents will help choose the timetable before bookings open.
The advice desk will explain public services and direct residents to qualified staff instead of making decisions on their behalf.
Planning for an ageing population will include varied interests rather than describe all older people as having identical needs.
A workshop on civic responsibility will explore both residents' contributions and the council's obligations.
Existing support networks will be invited to explain their work without sharing private details about members.
The mentoring discussion will examine social mobility while acknowledging barriers that individual advice cannot remove.
Small groups can build interpersonal trust through reliable shared tasks and clear ways to raise concerns.
A published booking policy will govern shared resources such as meeting rooms, tools and storage space.
Please recommend one change that we can explain and evaluate, while acknowledging what this proposal does not yet establish.
Regards, the planning coordinator
>>>
[/CONTENT]

[CONTENT ielts-society-community-discussion]
type: dialogue
text:
<<<
An (nam): Let us review this proposal from the perspective of people who will actually use it.
Lan (nữ): I agree. We should compare the intended benefits with practical barriers and decide what to ask the planning group.
An: Social cohesion develops when neighbours work together over time, not simply when they attend one large photograph session.
Lan: Voluntary work should be manageable alongside paid work, so short roles would help people with limited free time.
An: Social inclusion means I can choose a quieter event without being treated as less interested in the community.
Lan: Income inequality affects access to activities when equipment costs exclude people even if entry is free.
An: Community engagement would feel real if our suggestions could change the plan, not just decorate a completed proposal.
Lan: Public services can be confusing when each office uses different terms, so a clear signposting sheet would help.
An: An ageing population should not be seen only as a burden; older residents also bring skills and time.
Lan: Civic responsibility includes questioning a poor decision respectfully, not just accepting every official proposal.
An: Support networks should have clear boundaries so volunteers do not feel responsible for problems they cannot solve.
Lan: Social mobility requires opportunities as well as encouragement, so a confident speech alone will not solve unequal access.
An: Interpersonal trust would grow if organisers admitted mistakes and explained what they would do differently.
Lan: Shared resources need fair booking rules, otherwise the same confident group may take every popular time slot.
An: We have several reasonable priorities, but the budget cannot cover every suggestion immediately.
Lan: Let us recommend one change, explain our reasons, and identify evidence that would tell us whether it worked.
>>>
[/CONTENT]
