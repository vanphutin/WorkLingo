[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-affordable-housing]]
expression: affordable housing
meaning_vi: nhà ở có giá phù hợp khả năng chi trả
pronunciation: /əˈfɔːdəbəl ˈhaʊzɪŋ/
collocations: provide affordable housing
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The proposal reserves affordable housing for rent and requires the eligibility rules to be published.
common_error: Housing is uncountable; use homes for individual units.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-urban-planning]]
expression: urban planning
meaning_vi: quy hoạch đô thị
pronunciation: /ˈɜːbən ˈplænɪŋ/
collocations: effective urban planning
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The urban planning meeting will examine how the new homes connect to schools, shops and transport.
common_error: Planning is uncountable when naming the process.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-population-density]]
expression: population density
meaning_vi: mật độ dân số
pronunciation: /ˌpɒpjəˈleɪʃən ˈdensəti/
collocations: higher population density
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Higher population density may support local services, but the committee wants evidence about pressure on shared spaces.
common_error: Density refers to people per area, not population growth alone.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-green-spaces]]
expression: green spaces
meaning_vi: không gian xanh
pronunciation: /ɡriːn ˈspeɪsɪz/
collocations: protect urban green spaces
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The design keeps green spaces open to existing residents rather than fencing them for new tenants only.
common_error: Use spaces for separate parks and planted areas.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-rental-costs]]
expression: rental costs
meaning_vi: chi phí thuê nhà
pronunciation: /ˈrentəl kɒsts/
collocations: rising rental costs
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A comparison of rental costs will include heating and service charges, not just the advertised rent.
common_error: Rental is an adjective before costs; rent can be the payment itself.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-public-amenities]]
expression: public amenities
meaning_vi: tiện ích công cộng
pronunciation: /ˈpʌblɪk əˈmiːnətiz/
collocations: access to public amenities
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The developer must explain how public amenities will be funded after construction ends.
common_error: Amenities usually appears in the plural for useful shared facilities.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-mixed-use-development]]
expression: mixed-use development
meaning_vi: khu phát triển có nhiều chức năng
pronunciation: /ˌmɪkst juːs dɪˈveləpmənt/
collocations: a mixed-use development
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The mixed-use development combines homes with small shops, while delivery hours are limited to reduce disturbance.
common_error: Use a before one development project.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-building-maintenance]]
expression: building maintenance
meaning_vi: bảo trì tòa nhà
pronunciation: /ˈbɪldɪŋ ˈmeɪntənəns/
collocations: regular building maintenance
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Tenants will receive a building maintenance schedule and a named contact for urgent repairs.
common_error: Maintenance is uncountable; maintain is the verb.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-community-facilities]]
expression: community facilities
meaning_vi: cơ sở phục vụ cộng đồng
pronunciation: /kəˈmjuːnəti fəˈsɪlətiz/
collocations: shared community facilities
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The ground floor will include community facilities that local groups can book at a reduced charge.
common_error: Facilities is plural when naming several shared services or spaces.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-housing-shortage]]
expression: housing shortage
meaning_vi: tình trạng thiếu nhà ở
pronunciation: /ˈhaʊzɪŋ ˈʃɔːtɪdʒ/
collocations: address a housing shortage
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The report recognizes the housing shortage but does not treat every objection as opposition to new homes.
common_error: Use a for one identified shortage.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-neighbourhood-identity]]
expression: neighbourhood identity
meaning_vi: bản sắc khu dân cư
pronunciation: /ˈneɪbəhʊd aɪˈdentəti/
collocations: preserve neighbourhood identity
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Interviews about neighbourhood identity will include renters and recent arrivals as well as long-term homeowners.
common_error: Identity concerns shared character, not proof-of-address documents.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-housing-cities-long-term-investment]]
expression: long-term investment
meaning_vi: đầu tư dài hạn
pronunciation: /ˌlɒŋ tɜːm ɪnˈvestmənt/
collocations: secure long-term investment
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A long-term investment plan will set aside money for drainage and maintenance after the opening ceremony.
common_error: Hyphenate long-term when it comes before investment.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[CONTENT ielts-housing-cities-proposal]
type: email
text:
<<<
Dear planning group,
We are a residents association responding to a housing development proposal. Please compare the priorities below before our next meeting.
The proposal reserves affordable housing for rent and requires the eligibility rules to be published.
The urban planning meeting will examine how the new homes connect to schools, shops and transport.
Higher population density may support local services, but the committee wants evidence about pressure on shared spaces.
The design keeps green spaces open to existing residents rather than fencing them for new tenants only.
A comparison of rental costs will include heating and service charges, not just the advertised rent.
The developer must explain how public amenities will be funded after construction ends.
The mixed-use development combines homes with small shops, while delivery hours are limited to reduce disturbance.
Tenants will receive a building maintenance schedule and a named contact for urgent repairs.
The ground floor will include community facilities that local groups can book at a reduced charge.
The report recognizes the housing shortage but does not treat every objection as opposition to new homes.
Interviews about neighbourhood identity will include renters and recent arrivals as well as long-term homeowners.
A long-term investment plan will set aside money for drainage and maintenance after the opening ceremony.
Please recommend one change that we can explain and evaluate, while acknowledging what this proposal does not yet establish.
Regards, the planning coordinator
>>>
[/CONTENT]

[CONTENT ielts-housing-cities-discussion]
type: dialogue
text:
<<<
An (nam): Let us review this proposal from the perspective of people who will actually use it.
Lan (nữ): I agree. We should compare the intended benefits with practical barriers and decide what to ask the planning group.
An: Affordable housing would help my family, but we need clear information about deposits as well as monthly rent.
Lan: Urban planning should consider the walk to school before deciding where the car park will go.
An: Population density does not tell us whether a street feels crowded unless we also understand its layout.
Lan: Green spaces should have benches and clear entrances so older neighbours can use them comfortably.
An: Rental costs are hard to compare when one listing includes heating and another hides it in extra charges.
Lan: Public amenities need a maintenance budget; a new play area is less useful if broken equipment stays unrepaired.
An: A mixed-use development could let me buy groceries nearby, provided deliveries do not continue all night.
Lan: Building maintenance should include the shared lift, because a failure can isolate residents on upper floors.
An: Community facilities would be useful for our reading group if booking rules were simple and published in advance.
Lan: The housing shortage is real for families on the waiting list, so we should propose workable changes rather than reject everything.
An: Neighbourhood identity should reflect people who live here now, including recent arrivals, not only an old photograph.
Lan: Long-term investment matters because a project should work ten years after its attractive launch brochure is forgotten.
An: We have several reasonable priorities, but the budget cannot cover every suggestion immediately.
Lan: Let us recommend one change, explain our reasons, and identify evidence that would tell us whether it worked.
>>>
[/CONTENT]
