title: Technology in context

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-digital-literacy]]
expression: digital literacy
meaning_vi: năng lực sử dụng công nghệ số
pronunciation: /ˈdɪdʒɪtəl ˈlɪtərəsi/
collocations: improve digital literacy
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Our library will offer digital literacy sessions before introducing online-only booking for any service.
common_error: Literacy names an ability, not a particular device.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-data-privacy]]
expression: data privacy
meaning_vi: quyền riêng tư dữ liệu
pronunciation: /ˈdeɪtə ˈprɪvəsi/
collocations: protect data privacy
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The data privacy review recommends collecting only the details necessary to reserve a room.
common_error: Privacy is uncountable; avoid a privacy in this sense.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-artificial-intelligence]]
expression: artificial intelligence
meaning_vi: trí tuệ nhân tạo
pronunciation: /ˌɑːtɪˈfɪʃəl ɪnˈtelɪdʒəns/
collocations: use artificial intelligence
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A trial of artificial intelligence may help sort enquiries, but a librarian will check suggested replies.
common_error: Intelligence is uncountable when naming the technology.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-automation-tools]]
expression: automation tools
meaning_vi: công cụ tự động hóa
pronunciation: /ˌɔːtəˈmeɪʃən tuːlz/
collocations: adopt automation tools
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Simple automation tools will send reminders, while staff remain responsible for cancellations and exceptions.
common_error: Automation names the process; automatic is an adjective.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-reliable-information]]
expression: reliable information
meaning_vi: thông tin đáng tin cậy
pronunciation: /rɪˈlaɪəbəl ˌɪnfəˈmeɪʃən/
collocations: find reliable information
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The website will link reliable information to named sources and display when each page was checked.
common_error: Information is uncountable; avoid informations.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-online-security]]
expression: online security
meaning_vi: an toàn trên môi trường trực tuyến
pronunciation: /ˌɒnlaɪn sɪˈkjʊərəti/
collocations: improve online security
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Staff will explain online security without asking visitors to reveal their passwords during a workshop.
common_error: Security is not a countable password or device.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-user-experience]]
expression: user experience
meaning_vi: trải nghiệm người dùng
pronunciation: /ˈjuːzər ɪkˈspɪəriəns/
collocations: improve the user experience
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The user experience test will include people using screen readers and people with slow connections.
common_error: Use experience as a noun after user.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-technological-change]]
expression: technological change
meaning_vi: sự thay đổi công nghệ
pronunciation: /ˌteknəˈlɒdʒɪkəl tʃeɪndʒ/
collocations: adapt to technological change
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Training time is included in the budget because technological change affects daily routines as well as equipment.
common_error: Technological modifies change; technology is the noun.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-remote-access]]
expression: remote access
meaning_vi: khả năng truy cập từ xa
pronunciation: /rɪˈməʊt ˈækses/
collocations: provide remote access
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The proposal provides remote access to selected resources, while print copies remain available in the building.
common_error: Access usually takes to before the service being accessed.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-digital-divide]]
expression: digital divide
meaning_vi: khoảng cách trong tiếp cận công nghệ số
pronunciation: /ˈdɪdʒɪtəl dɪˈvaɪd/
collocations: bridge the digital divide
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Loan devices and telephone support are intended to reduce the digital divide rather than assume everyone is connected.
common_error: Use the digital divide when naming the social gap.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-technical-support]]
expression: technical support
meaning_vi: hỗ trợ kỹ thuật
pronunciation: /ˈteknɪkəl səˈpɔːt/
collocations: provide technical support
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A named member of staff will provide technical support during the first month of the trial.
common_error: Support is uncountable here.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-technology-screen-time]]
expression: screen time
meaning_vi: thời gian sử dụng màn hình
pronunciation: /skriːn taɪm/
collocations: manage screen time
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Feedback will distinguish useful screen time from avoidable repeated logins and confusing navigation.
common_error: Time is uncountable for a duration, not a number of occasions.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[/WORD_BANK]

[CONTENT ielts-technology-proposal]
type: email
text:
<<<
Dear planning group,
We are a library choosing a digital service for residents. Please compare the priorities below before our next meeting.
Our library will offer digital literacy sessions before introducing online-only booking for any service.
The data privacy review recommends collecting only the details necessary to reserve a room.
A trial of artificial intelligence may help sort enquiries, but a librarian will check suggested replies.
Simple automation tools will send reminders, while staff remain responsible for cancellations and exceptions.
The website will link reliable information to named sources and display when each page was checked.
Staff will explain online security without asking visitors to reveal their passwords during a workshop.
The user experience test will include people using screen readers and people with slow connections.
Training time is included in the budget because technological change affects daily routines as well as equipment.
The proposal provides remote access to selected resources, while print copies remain available in the building.
Loan devices and telephone support are intended to reduce the digital divide rather than assume everyone is connected.
A named member of staff will provide technical support during the first month of the trial.
Feedback will distinguish useful screen time from avoidable repeated logins and confusing navigation.
Please recommend one change that we can explain and evaluate, while acknowledging what this proposal does not yet establish.
Regards, the planning coordinator
>>>
[/CONTENT]

[CONTENT ielts-technology-discussion]
type: dialogue
text:
<<<
An (nam): Let us review this proposal from the perspective of people who will actually use it.
Lan (nữ): I agree. We should compare the intended benefits with practical barriers and decide what to ask the planning group.
An: Digital literacy classes would help my father, but he needs practice on the device he actually owns.
Lan: Data privacy matters to me because reserving a desk should not require my employment history.
An: Artificial intelligence could save time, provided a person checks inaccurate or unsuitable suggestions.
Lan: Automation tools should let a member stop reminders easily rather than send repeated messages they cannot control.
An: I judge reliable information by its evidence and source, not by how professional the page looks.
Lan: Online security advice should include checking a suspicious message before opening its attachment.
An: A good user experience would let me find the cancellation button without asking a staff member.
Lan: Technological change feels less disruptive when we can try a system before the old one disappears.
An: Remote access would help me use the library after work, but I still want help when a page fails.
Lan: The digital divide concerns confidence as well as equipment; owning a phone does not mean understanding every form.
An: Technical support should be easy to contact, with a telephone alternative when the website itself is unavailable.
Lan: I do not want extra screen time just to renew a book that used to take one short call.
An: We have several reasonable priorities, but the budget cannot cover every suggestion immediately.
Lan: Let us recommend one change, explain our reasons, and identify evidence that would tell us whether it worked.
>>>
[/CONTENT]
