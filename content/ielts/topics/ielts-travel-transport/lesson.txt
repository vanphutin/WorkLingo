[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-traffic-congestion]]
expression: traffic congestion
meaning_vi: tình trạng ùn tắc giao thông
pronunciation: /ˈtræfɪk kənˈdʒestʃən/
collocations: reduce traffic congestion
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Our route study measures traffic congestion at school opening times rather than relying on a daily average.
common_error: Congestion is uncountable; do not say many congestions.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-commuting-time]]
expression: commuting time
meaning_vi: thời gian đi lại thường ngày
pronunciation: /kəˈmjuːtɪŋ taɪm/
collocations: reduce commuting time
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A staff survey will record commuting time from door to door, including walks and transfers.
common_error: Commuting concerns regular travel between home and work or study.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-transport-infrastructure]]
expression: transport infrastructure
meaning_vi: cơ sở hạ tầng giao thông
pronunciation: /ˈtrænspɔːt ˈɪnfrəstrʌktʃə/
collocations: invest in transport infrastructure
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The transport infrastructure budget includes pavements and shelters as well as vehicles.
common_error: Infrastructure is normally uncountable.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-pedestrian-safety]]
expression: pedestrian safety
meaning_vi: an toàn cho người đi bộ
pronunciation: /pəˈdestriən ˈseɪfti/
collocations: improve pedestrian safety
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A crossing near the clinic is the first pedestrian safety proposal because residents report difficulty reaching the stop.
common_error: Pedestrian modifies safety and stays singular in the phrase.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-affordable-fares]]
expression: affordable fares
meaning_vi: giá vé phải chăng
pronunciation: /əˈfɔːdəbəl feəz/
collocations: offer affordable fares
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The council proposes affordable fares through a weekly cap instead of a discount limited to smartphone users.
common_error: Fares are transport prices; fees usually refer to other services.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-peak-hours]]
expression: peak hours
meaning_vi: giờ cao điểm
pronunciation: /piːk ˈaʊəz/
collocations: travel during peak hours
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Extra buses during peak hours will be tested before the team considers reducing quieter services.
common_error: Use during peak hours, not in peak hour when referring generally.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-rail-connections]]
expression: rail connections
meaning_vi: các tuyến kết nối đường sắt
pronunciation: /reɪl kəˈnekʃənz/
collocations: improve rail connections
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The timetable will coordinate rail connections by allowing time for passengers to walk between platforms.
common_error: Connections can refer to services that link two journeys.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-active-travel]]
expression: active travel
meaning_vi: di chuyển bằng vận động cơ thể
pronunciation: /ˈæktɪv ˈtrævəl/
collocations: encourage active travel
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Secure cycle parking and wider pavements are proposed to encourage active travel on short local trips.
common_error: Travel is uncountable when naming walking and cycling as a policy area.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-service-reliability]]
expression: service reliability
meaning_vi: độ tin cậy của dịch vụ
pronunciation: /ˈsɜːvɪs rɪˌlaɪəˈbɪləti/
collocations: improve service reliability
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Published service reliability figures will include cancellations, not only the punctuality of buses that ran.
common_error: Reliability is a noun; reliable is an adjective.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-tourist-destinations]]
expression: tourist destinations
meaning_vi: điểm đến du lịch
pronunciation: /ˈtʊərɪst ˌdestɪˈneɪʃənz/
collocations: popular tourist destinations
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Summer routes to tourist destinations will be funded separately from the core services residents use throughout the year.
common_error: Destination is countable, so pluralize it for several places.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-accessible-routes]]
expression: accessible routes
meaning_vi: tuyến đường dễ tiếp cận cho nhiều đối tượng
pronunciation: /əkˈsesəbəl ruːts/
collocations: design accessible routes
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Auditors will test accessible routes with wheelchair users before describing the network as fully accessible.
common_error: Accessible concerns usability, not just whether a road exists.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-travel-transport-travel-demand]]
expression: travel demand
meaning_vi: nhu cầu đi lại
pronunciation: /ˈtrævəl dɪˈmɑːnd/
collocations: forecast travel demand
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Counts in different seasons will help forecast travel demand and avoid treating a festival weekend as typical.
common_error: Demand is uncountable for overall need.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[CONTENT ielts-travel-transport-proposal]
type: email
text:
<<<
Dear planning group,
We are a transport team comparing changes to a town bus network. Please compare the priorities below before our next meeting.
Our route study measures traffic congestion at school opening times rather than relying on a daily average.
A staff survey will record commuting time from door to door, including walks and transfers.
The transport infrastructure budget includes pavements and shelters as well as vehicles.
A crossing near the clinic is the first pedestrian safety proposal because residents report difficulty reaching the stop.
The council proposes affordable fares through a weekly cap instead of a discount limited to smartphone users.
Extra buses during peak hours will be tested before the team considers reducing quieter services.
The timetable will coordinate rail connections by allowing time for passengers to walk between platforms.
Secure cycle parking and wider pavements are proposed to encourage active travel on short local trips.
Published service reliability figures will include cancellations, not only the punctuality of buses that ran.
Summer routes to tourist destinations will be funded separately from the core services residents use throughout the year.
Auditors will test accessible routes with wheelchair users before describing the network as fully accessible.
Counts in different seasons will help forecast travel demand and avoid treating a festival weekend as typical.
Please recommend one change that we can explain and evaluate, while acknowledging what this proposal does not yet establish.
Regards, the planning coordinator
>>>
[/CONTENT]

[CONTENT ielts-travel-transport-discussion]
type: dialogue
text:
<<<
An (nam): Let us review this proposal from the perspective of people who will actually use it.
Lan (nữ): I agree. We should compare the intended benefits with practical barriers and decide what to ask the planning group.
An: Traffic congestion affects my morning trip, but the road is usually quiet when I travel home at night.
Lan: My commuting time includes waiting for a second bus, so a faster first journey may not solve the problem.
An: Transport infrastructure should include a safe pavement to the stop, not only a new bus.
Lan: Pedestrian safety is my priority because my mother must cross a busy road to reach the clinic bus.
An: Affordable fares would help occasional workers, especially if the price cap also applied to cash payments.
Lan: I work outside peak hours, so I hope improving the morning service will not remove the last evening bus.
An: Rail connections are stressful when a timetable allows only two minutes to change platforms with luggage.
Lan: Active travel is realistic for my short shopping trip if there is somewhere secure to leave a bicycle.
An: Service reliability matters more to me than an advertised journey time that ignores frequent cancellations.
Lan: Tourist destinations need signs, but local commuters should not lose their regular route whenever visitor numbers rise.
An: Accessible routes should account for broken lifts, because a nominally step-free station can still be unusable.
Lan: Travel demand changes during school holidays, so we should compare similar weeks before judging the new timetable.
An: We have several reasonable priorities, but the budget cannot cover every suggestion immediately.
Lan: Let us recommend one change, explain our reasons, and identify evidence that would tell us whether it worked.
>>>
[/CONTENT]
