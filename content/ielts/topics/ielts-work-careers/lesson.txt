[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-job-satisfaction]]
expression: job satisfaction
meaning_vi: sự hài lòng trong công việc
pronunciation: /dʒɒb ˌsætɪsˈfækʃən/
collocations: improve job satisfaction
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Our anonymous staff survey links job satisfaction to useful feedback, not just a higher salary.
common_error: Use satisfaction as an uncountable noun here.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-career-prospects]]
expression: career prospects
meaning_vi: triển vọng nghề nghiệp
pronunciation: /kəˈrɪə ˈprɒspekts/
collocations: better career prospects
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The mentoring scheme should improve career prospects by making promotion requirements visible to everyone.
common_error: Use prospects in the plural for future opportunities.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-work-life-balance]]
expression: work-life balance
meaning_vi: cân bằng công việc và cuộc sống
pronunciation: /ˌwɜːk laɪf ˈbæləns/
collocations: maintain work-life balance
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Flexible start times may support work-life balance, but staff still need a reliable handover each day.
common_error: Use balance, not balancing, after work-life in this noun phrase.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-professional-development]]
expression: professional development
meaning_vi: phát triển chuyên môn
pronunciation: /prəˈfeʃənəl dɪˈveləpmənt/
collocations: ongoing professional development
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A paid afternoon each month will be reserved for professional development chosen with a supervisor.
common_error: Development is normally uncountable in this use.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-transferable-skills]]
expression: transferable skills
meaning_vi: kỹ năng có thể áp dụng sang lĩnh vực khác
pronunciation: /trænsˈfɜːrəbəl skɪlz/
collocations: demonstrate transferable skills
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Recruiters will ask applicants to demonstrate transferable skills with examples from work, study or volunteering.
common_error: Transferable describes a skill usable in a different role.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-job-security]]
expression: job security
meaning_vi: sự ổn định việc làm
pronunciation: /dʒɒb sɪˈkjʊərəti/
collocations: greater job security
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The pilot will not change contracts, and staff will receive a written explanation of job security during the trial.
common_error: Do not use security to mean a guaranteed promotion.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-flexible-working]]
expression: flexible working
meaning_vi: làm việc linh hoạt
pronunciation: /ˈfleksəbəl ˈwɜːkɪŋ/
collocations: introduce flexible working
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The flexible working pilot will last three months and include both office and home-based tasks.
common_error: Working is the noun here; avoid flexible work-time when naming this policy.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-team-collaboration]]
expression: team collaboration
meaning_vi: sự phối hợp trong nhóm
pronunciation: /tiːm kəˌlæbəˈreɪʃən/
collocations: effective team collaboration
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A shared project board will support team collaboration by showing who owns each unresolved task.
common_error: Collaboration means working together, not competition.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-performance-review]]
expression: performance review
meaning_vi: buổi đánh giá hiệu quả công việc
pronunciation: /pəˈfɔːməns rɪˈvjuː/
collocations: a constructive performance review
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Each performance review will include a development goal rather than only a numerical rating.
common_error: A review is countable when it means one scheduled meeting.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-staff-turnover]]
expression: staff turnover
meaning_vi: tỷ lệ nhân viên nghỉ và thay thế
pronunciation: /stɑːf ˈtɜːnəʊvə/
collocations: reduce staff turnover
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: We will compare staff turnover before and after the pilot, while recognizing that pay changes may also affect it.
common_error: Turnover here concerns employees leaving, not sales revenue.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-workplace-culture]]
expression: workplace culture
meaning_vi: văn hóa nơi làm việc
pronunciation: /ˈwɜːkpleɪs ˈkʌltʃə/
collocations: an inclusive workplace culture
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Managers must model the workplace culture they describe by respecting agreed boundaries on evening messages.
common_error: Workplace is one word when used as a noun modifier.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-work-careers-income-stability]]
expression: income stability
meaning_vi: sự ổn định thu nhập
pronunciation: /ˈɪnkʌm stəˈbɪləti/
collocations: protect income stability
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Hourly workers will keep their guaranteed shifts so that the pilot does not reduce income stability.
common_error: Stability refers to predictability, not necessarily high income.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[/WORD_BANK]

[CONTENT ielts-work-careers-proposal]
type: email
text:
<<<
Dear planning group,
We are a small employer reviewing a flexible working pilot. Please compare the priorities below before our next meeting.
Our anonymous staff survey links job satisfaction to useful feedback, not just a higher salary.
The mentoring scheme should improve career prospects by making promotion requirements visible to everyone.
Flexible start times may support work-life balance, but staff still need a reliable handover each day.
A paid afternoon each month will be reserved for professional development chosen with a supervisor.
Recruiters will ask applicants to demonstrate transferable skills with examples from work, study or volunteering.
The pilot will not change contracts, and staff will receive a written explanation of job security during the trial.
The flexible working pilot will last three months and include both office and home-based tasks.
A shared project board will support team collaboration by showing who owns each unresolved task.
Each performance review will include a development goal rather than only a numerical rating.
We will compare staff turnover before and after the pilot, while recognizing that pay changes may also affect it.
Managers must model the workplace culture they describe by respecting agreed boundaries on evening messages.
Hourly workers will keep their guaranteed shifts so that the pilot does not reduce income stability.
Please recommend one change that we can explain and evaluate, while acknowledging what this proposal does not yet establish.
Regards, the planning coordinator
>>>
[/CONTENT]

[CONTENT ielts-work-careers-discussion]
type: dialogue
text:
<<<
An (nam): Let us review this proposal from the perspective of people who will actually use it.
Lan (nữ): I agree. We should compare the intended benefits with practical barriers and decide what to ask the planning group.
An: My job satisfaction increased when my manager explained how my work helped the wider team.
Lan: I worry about my career prospects because I do not understand the requirements for a senior role.
An: My work-life balance would improve if I could start early and collect my daughter after school.
Lan: Professional development should include customer communication, not only new software certificates.
An: I used transferable skills from volunteering when I had to organize a busy delivery team.
Lan: Before joining the pilot, I need reassurance about job security rather than a vague promise about growth.
An: Flexible working sounds useful, but some repairs still require equipment that stays in the office.
Lan: Team collaboration would be easier if everyone updated the board before the afternoon handover.
An: Could my performance review include feedback from colleagues who work with me every day?
Lan: Lower staff turnover could save time, but we should ask people why they left instead of guessing.
An: A supportive workplace culture should allow a new colleague to ask for help without embarrassment.
Lan: Income stability matters to me because a predictable monthly amount helps me plan rent and transport.
An: We have several reasonable priorities, but the budget cannot cover every suggestion immediately.
Lan: Let us recommend one change, explain our reasons, and identify evidence that would tell us whether it worked.
>>>
[/CONTENT]
