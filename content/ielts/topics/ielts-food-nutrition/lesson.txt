[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-food-security]]
expression: food security
meaning_vi: an ninh lương thực
pronunciation: /fuːd sɪˈkjʊərəti/
collocations: improve food security
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Our kitchen's food security discussion considers whether households can reliably obtain suitable food throughout the month.
common_error: Security refers to reliable access here, not guards at a food shop.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-nutritional-value]]
expression: nutritional value
meaning_vi: giá trị dinh dưỡng
pronunciation: /njuːˈtrɪʃənəl ˈvæljuː/
collocations: compare nutritional value
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The menu exercise compares nutritional value using labelled fictional products rather than promoting a particular brand.
common_error: Nutritional describes food content; nutritious describes food that nourishes.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-local-produce]]
expression: local produce
meaning_vi: nông sản địa phương
pronunciation: /ˈləʊkəl ˈprɒdjuːs/
collocations: buy local produce
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: We will buy local produce when price, availability and storage needs fit the kitchen's weekly plan.
common_error: Produce is uncountable when it means agricultural products.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-seasonal-ingredients]]
expression: seasonal ingredients
meaning_vi: nguyên liệu theo mùa
pronunciation: /ˈsiːzənəl ɪnˈɡriːdiənts/
collocations: use seasonal ingredients
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A flexible recipe list will use seasonal ingredients without assuming that every familiar dish must be changed.
common_error: Seasonal refers to availability at particular times of year.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-food-waste]]
expression: food waste
meaning_vi: lãng phí thực phẩm
pronunciation: /fuːd weɪst/
collocations: reduce food waste
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Staff will measure food waste from preparation and uneaten portions separately to understand where changes are needed.
common_error: Waste is uncountable in this phrase.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-dietary-requirements]]
expression: dietary requirements
meaning_vi: nhu cầu ăn uống đặc thù
pronunciation: /ˈdaɪətəri rɪˈkwaɪəmənts/
collocations: meet dietary requirements
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Booking forms will ask about dietary requirements while limiting access to any sensitive information supplied.
common_error: Use requirements for multiple needs; do not treat preferences as diagnoses.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-processed-food]]
expression: processed food
meaning_vi: thực phẩm đã qua chế biến
pronunciation: /ˈprəʊsest fuːd/
collocations: compare processed food
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The comparison of processed food will consider ingredients, storage and convenience rather than use the label as a complete judgement.
common_error: Processed describes preparation; avoid assuming it always means one nutritional profile.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-sustainable-agriculture]]
expression: sustainable agriculture
meaning_vi: nông nghiệp bền vững
pronunciation: /səˈsteɪnəbəl ˈæɡrɪkʌltʃə/
collocations: support sustainable agriculture
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Suppliers will be asked to explain sustainable agriculture claims with specific practices rather than decorative logos.
common_error: Agriculture is uncountable when naming the sector.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-portion-sizes]]
expression: portion sizes
meaning_vi: khẩu phần ăn
pronunciation: /ˈpɔːʃən saɪzɪz/
collocations: adjust portion sizes
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Guests may choose portion sizes and request more, allowing the kitchen to compare waste without leaving people hungry.
common_error: Portion is a noun modifier; pluralize sizes for several serving amounts.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-food-supply-chain]]
expression: food supply chain
meaning_vi: chuỗi cung ứng thực phẩm
pronunciation: /fuːd səˈplaɪ tʃeɪn/
collocations: a resilient food supply chain
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Mapping the food supply chain will show where delivery delays could disrupt the weekly menu.
common_error: Use a before one chain linking production and delivery.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-consumer-preferences]]
expression: consumer preferences
meaning_vi: sở thích người tiêu dùng
pronunciation: /kənˈsjuːmə ˈprefərənsɪz/
collocations: understand consumer preferences
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The survey of consumer preferences will allow open comments instead of asking guests to choose only between two unfamiliar dishes.
common_error: Preferences are choices people favour, not necessarily requirements.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-food-nutrition-meal-planning]]
expression: meal planning
meaning_vi: việc lên kế hoạch bữa ăn
pronunciation: /miːl ˈplænɪŋ/
collocations: weekly meal planning
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Weekly meal planning will connect the number of bookings with purchasing decisions and safe storage capacity.
common_error: Planning is uncountable when naming an activity.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[CONTENT ielts-food-nutrition-proposal]
type: email
text:
<<<
Dear planning group,
We are a community kitchen comparing affordable menu and purchasing options. Please compare the priorities below before our next meeting.
Our kitchen's food security discussion considers whether households can reliably obtain suitable food throughout the month.
The menu exercise compares nutritional value using labelled fictional products rather than promoting a particular brand.
We will buy local produce when price, availability and storage needs fit the kitchen's weekly plan.
A flexible recipe list will use seasonal ingredients without assuming that every familiar dish must be changed.
Staff will measure food waste from preparation and uneaten portions separately to understand where changes are needed.
Booking forms will ask about dietary requirements while limiting access to any sensitive information supplied.
The comparison of processed food will consider ingredients, storage and convenience rather than use the label as a complete judgement.
Suppliers will be asked to explain sustainable agriculture claims with specific practices rather than decorative logos.
Guests may choose portion sizes and request more, allowing the kitchen to compare waste without leaving people hungry.
Mapping the food supply chain will show where delivery delays could disrupt the weekly menu.
The survey of consumer preferences will allow open comments instead of asking guests to choose only between two unfamiliar dishes.
Weekly meal planning will connect the number of bookings with purchasing decisions and safe storage capacity.
Please recommend one change that we can explain and evaluate, while acknowledging what this proposal does not yet establish.
Regards, the planning coordinator
>>>
[/CONTENT]

[CONTENT ielts-food-nutrition-discussion]
type: dialogue
text:
<<<
An (nam): Let us review this proposal from the perspective of people who will actually use it.
Lan (nữ): I agree. We should compare the intended benefits with practical barriers and decide what to ask the planning group.
An: Food security concerns choice and consistency, so a one-off donation does not answer every household's needs.
Lan: Nutritional value is useful to compare, but serving size must be clear or two labels can appear misleadingly different.
An: Local produce is attractive, but a small supplier needs predictable orders rather than last-minute demands.
Lan: Seasonal ingredients could help us vary the menu if cooks are given alternatives when a delivery is unavailable.
An: Reducing food waste requires knowing why food was left; smaller portions are not the answer if the recipe was unpopular.
Lan: Dietary requirements should be discussed respectfully, and guests should not have to explain private details to everyone at the table.
An: Processed food can be convenient for busy households, so our discussion should examine actual labels instead of making assumptions.
Lan: Sustainable agriculture claims should identify what a farm does and what evidence supports that description.
An: Different portion sizes would let me choose a smaller first serving while knowing I could ask for more.
Lan: A food supply chain map should include storage and transport, not only the farm and the final kitchen.
An: Consumer preferences may change after people try a dish, so a tasting event could complement a written survey.
Lan: Meal planning would be easier if booking changes reached the cooks before they placed the final order.
An: We have several reasonable priorities, but the budget cannot cover every suggestion immediately.
Lan: Let us recommend one change, explain our reasons, and identify evidence that would tell us whether it worked.
>>>
[/CONTENT]
