[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-target-audience]]
expression: target audience
meaning_vi: đối tượng công chúng mục tiêu
pronunciation: /ˈtɑːɡɪt ˈɔːdiəns/
collocations: identify a target audience
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Our target audience is residents seeking practical local information, including people who do not use social media.
common_error: Audience refers to a group; avoid audiences when describing one group here.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-advertising-revenue]]
expression: advertising revenue
meaning_vi: doanh thu quảng cáo
pronunciation: /ˈædvətaɪzɪŋ ˈrevənjuː/
collocations: generate advertising revenue
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Limited advertising revenue could fund reporting, but sponsors will not choose which stories are investigated.
common_error: Revenue is income, not profit after costs.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-media-coverage]]
expression: media coverage
meaning_vi: sự đưa tin của truyền thông
pronunciation: /ˈmiːdiə ˈkʌvərɪdʒ/
collocations: balanced media coverage
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The editor wants media coverage to include routine council decisions as well as dramatic events.
common_error: Coverage is normally uncountable in this meaning.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-brand-awareness]]
expression: brand awareness
meaning_vi: mức độ nhận biết thương hiệu
pronunciation: /brænd əˈweənəs/
collocations: raise brand awareness
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A sponsor may gain brand awareness through a clearly marked advertisement beside an article.
common_error: Use raise, not rise, with a direct object.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-misleading-claims]]
expression: misleading claims
meaning_vi: những tuyên bố gây hiểu lầm
pronunciation: /mɪsˈliːdɪŋ kleɪmz/
collocations: challenge misleading claims
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Editors will reject misleading claims when advertisers cannot provide the evidence supporting their promises.
common_error: Misleading describes the claim; misled describes a person affected.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-editorial-independence]]
expression: editorial independence
meaning_vi: tính độc lập biên tập
pronunciation: /ˌedɪˈtɔːriəl ˌɪndɪˈpendəns/
collocations: protect editorial independence
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The funding agreement will protect editorial independence by separating sponsorship decisions from story selection.
common_error: Independence is a noun; independent is an adjective.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-consumer-behaviour]]
expression: consumer behaviour
meaning_vi: hành vi người tiêu dùng
pronunciation: /kənˈsjuːmə bɪˈheɪvjə/
collocations: influence consumer behaviour
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A class survey on consumer behaviour will ask what influenced a purchase without collecting participants' full shopping histories.
common_error: Behaviour is normally uncountable in British English.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-sponsored-content]]
expression: sponsored content
meaning_vi: nội dung được tài trợ
pronunciation: /ˈspɒnsəd ˈkɒntent/
collocations: clearly labelled sponsored content
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: All sponsored content will carry a label at the top rather than hide the relationship in a separate policy page.
common_error: Content is uncountable when referring to media material.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-source-credibility]]
expression: source credibility
meaning_vi: độ tin cậy của nguồn tin
pronunciation: /sɔːs ˌkredəˈbɪləti/
collocations: assess source credibility
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Reporters will assess source credibility by checking expertise, evidence and relevant conflicts of interest.
common_error: Credibility is not the same as popularity.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-public-opinion]]
expression: public opinion
meaning_vi: dư luận công chúng
pronunciation: /ˌpʌblɪk əˈpɪnjən/
collocations: shape public opinion
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A voluntary comment poll will not be presented as representative public opinion because participation is self-selected.
common_error: Opinion can be uncountable when describing a broad social view.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-digital-footprint]]
expression: digital footprint
meaning_vi: dấu vết số
pronunciation: /ˈdɪdʒɪtəl ˈfʊtprɪnt/
collocations: manage a digital footprint
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The project will explain its digital footprint policy and avoid unnecessary tracking of individual reading habits.
common_error: Use a before one person’s footprint.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-media-advertising-fact-checking]]
expression: fact checking
meaning_vi: kiểm chứng thông tin
pronunciation: /ˈfækt ˌtʃekɪŋ/
collocations: independent fact checking
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A second editor will complete fact checking before publication and keep a visible record of later corrections.
common_error: Checking is a noun here; check facts is the verb phrase.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[/WORD_BANK]

[CONTENT ielts-media-advertising-proposal]
type: email
text:
<<<
Dear planning group,
We are a student newsroom deciding how to fund a local information project. Please compare the priorities below before our next meeting.
Our target audience is residents seeking practical local information, including people who do not use social media.
Limited advertising revenue could fund reporting, but sponsors will not choose which stories are investigated.
The editor wants media coverage to include routine council decisions as well as dramatic events.
A sponsor may gain brand awareness through a clearly marked advertisement beside an article.
Editors will reject misleading claims when advertisers cannot provide the evidence supporting their promises.
The funding agreement will protect editorial independence by separating sponsorship decisions from story selection.
A class survey on consumer behaviour will ask what influenced a purchase without collecting participants' full shopping histories.
All sponsored content will carry a label at the top rather than hide the relationship in a separate policy page.
Reporters will assess source credibility by checking expertise, evidence and relevant conflicts of interest.
A voluntary comment poll will not be presented as representative public opinion because participation is self-selected.
The project will explain its digital footprint policy and avoid unnecessary tracking of individual reading habits.
A second editor will complete fact checking before publication and keep a visible record of later corrections.
Please recommend one change that we can explain and evaluate, while acknowledging what this proposal does not yet establish.
Regards, the planning coordinator
>>>
[/CONTENT]

[CONTENT ielts-media-advertising-discussion]
type: dialogue
text:
<<<
An (nam): Let us review this proposal from the perspective of people who will actually use it.
Lan (nữ): I agree. We should compare the intended benefits with practical barriers and decide what to ask the planning group.
An: The target audience should include new residents who may not recognize local place names in our headlines.
Lan: Advertising revenue may keep the newsletter free, yet I would not let a sponsor approve our criticism.
An: Media coverage of our estate usually focuses on problems, so a story about residents solving one would add context.
Lan: Brand awareness is a reasonable commercial aim, but an advertisement must not look like an independent recommendation.
An: Misleading claims can remain harmful even when a small disclaimer appears at the bottom of the page.
Lan: Editorial independence needs a written rule, because friendly requests from an important sponsor can still create pressure.
An: Consumer behaviour is influenced by convenience as well as price, so our survey should allow more than one reason.
Lan: Sponsored content should remain clearly labelled when readers share only a screenshot of the opening paragraph.
An: Source credibility matters more than a large follower count when a post makes a claim about public spending.
Lan: Public opinion cannot be inferred from the loudest ten comments if many readers choose not to post.
An: My digital footprint should not grow just because I read a local article without creating an account.
Lan: Fact checking should continue after publication when a reader supplies evidence that changes our understanding.
An: We have several reasonable priorities, but the budget cannot cover every suggestion immediately.
Lan: Let us recommend one change, explain our reasons, and identify evidence that would tell us whether it worked.
>>>
[/CONTENT]
