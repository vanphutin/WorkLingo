[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-public-health]]
expression: public health
meaning_vi: sức khỏe cộng đồng
pronunciation: /ˌpʌblɪk ˈhelθ/
collocations: improve public health
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Our public health programme will focus on access to information rather than offering personal medical treatment.
common_error: Public health concerns populations, not just public hospitals.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-balanced-diet]]
expression: balanced diet
meaning_vi: chế độ ăn cân đối
pronunciation: /ˌbælənst ˈdaɪət/
collocations: maintain a balanced diet
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A cooking session will explore a balanced diet using affordable ingredients selected by participants.
common_error: Use a with diet when describing one eating pattern.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-physical-activity]]
expression: physical activity
meaning_vi: hoạt động thể chất
pronunciation: /ˈfɪzɪkəl ækˈtɪvəti/
collocations: regular physical activity
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Residents can suggest physical activity sessions, including gentle walking and seated movement.
common_error: Activity can be uncountable when referring to movement in general.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-mental-wellbeing]]
expression: mental wellbeing
meaning_vi: sức khỏe tinh thần
pronunciation: /ˈmentəl ˌwelˈbiːɪŋ/
collocations: support mental wellbeing
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The mental wellbeing discussion will explain where residents can find qualified support when they need it.
common_error: Wellbeing is a noun; do not replace it with well when naming the concept.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-preventive-care]]
expression: preventive care
meaning_vi: chăm sóc dự phòng
pronunciation: /prɪˈventɪv keə/
collocations: access to preventive care
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A visiting service will explain preventive care appointments and how to ask the local clinic about eligibility.
common_error: Care is normally uncountable in this meaning.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-healthcare-access]]
expression: healthcare access
meaning_vi: khả năng tiếp cận dịch vụ y tế
pronunciation: /ˈhelθkeər ˈækses/
collocations: improve healthcare access
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A resident survey identifies transport and unclear booking instructions as barriers to healthcare access.
common_error: Access is a noun here; use access to a service.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-sedentary-lifestyle]]
expression: sedentary lifestyle
meaning_vi: lối sống ít vận động
pronunciation: /ˈsedəntəri ˈlaɪfstaɪl/
collocations: a sedentary lifestyle
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A workplace discussion will examine how long seated periods can contribute to a sedentary lifestyle.
common_error: Use a before the singular countable noun lifestyle.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-sleep-quality]]
expression: sleep quality
meaning_vi: chất lượng giấc ngủ
pronunciation: /sliːp ˈkwɒləti/
collocations: better sleep quality
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The sleep quality workshop will discuss daily routines without diagnosing participants or recommending medication.
common_error: Quality is a noun; avoid good-quality sleeping as the concept label.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-health-literacy]]
expression: health literacy
meaning_vi: khả năng hiểu và sử dụng thông tin sức khỏe
pronunciation: /helθ ˈlɪtərəsi/
collocations: develop health literacy
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: The health literacy activity asks groups to distinguish a source's evidence from its advertising claims.
common_error: Literacy is uncountable here.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-social-support]]
expression: social support
meaning_vi: sự hỗ trợ xã hội
pronunciation: /ˈsəʊʃəl səˈpɔːt/
collocations: a network of social support
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: A weekly tea group will provide social support without recording private details of members' conversations.
common_error: Support is uncountable in this phrase.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-work-related-stress]]
expression: work-related stress
meaning_vi: căng thẳng liên quan đến công việc
pronunciation: /ˌwɜːk rɪˌleɪtɪd ˈstres/
collocations: manage work-related stress
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Employers invited to the work-related stress forum must discuss workloads as well as individual coping routines.
common_error: Stress is usually uncountable when naming pressure.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[[LANGUAGE_BLOCK ielts-health-quality-of-life]]
expression: quality of life
meaning_vi: chất lượng cuộc sống
pronunciation: /ˌkwɒləti əv ˈlaɪf/
collocations: improve quality of life
grammar_pattern: Noun phrase: use as a subject, object, or complement after a suitable preposition.
example: Programme feedback will ask about quality of life and accessibility, rather than counting attendance as the only success.
common_error: Keep of between quality and life.
[[/LANGUAGE_BLOCK]]

[CONTENT ielts-health-proposal]
type: email
text:
<<<
Dear planning group,
We are a community centre designing a fictional wellbeing programme. Please compare the priorities below before our next meeting.
Our public health programme will focus on access to information rather than offering personal medical treatment.
A cooking session will explore a balanced diet using affordable ingredients selected by participants.
Residents can suggest physical activity sessions, including gentle walking and seated movement.
The mental wellbeing discussion will explain where residents can find qualified support when they need it.
A visiting service will explain preventive care appointments and how to ask the local clinic about eligibility.
A resident survey identifies transport and unclear booking instructions as barriers to healthcare access.
A workplace discussion will examine how long seated periods can contribute to a sedentary lifestyle.
The sleep quality workshop will discuss daily routines without diagnosing participants or recommending medication.
The health literacy activity asks groups to distinguish a source's evidence from its advertising claims.
A weekly tea group will provide social support without recording private details of members' conversations.
Employers invited to the work-related stress forum must discuss workloads as well as individual coping routines.
Programme feedback will ask about quality of life and accessibility, rather than counting attendance as the only success.
Please recommend one change that we can explain and evaluate, while acknowledging what this proposal does not yet establish.
Regards, the planning coordinator
>>>
[/CONTENT]

[CONTENT ielts-health-discussion]
type: dialogue
text:
<<<
An (nam): Let us review this proposal from the perspective of people who will actually use it.
Lan (nữ): I agree. We should compare the intended benefits with practical barriers and decide what to ask the planning group.
An: Public health information should be easy to understand, especially for people who are new to the area.
Lan: A balanced diet session would be useful if it respected different budgets and cultural food preferences.
An: I would join physical activity in a small group, but a competitive event might discourage me.
Lan: Mental wellbeing matters, but volunteers should know the limits of their role and refer specialist questions.
An: Clear information about preventive care would help me understand which appointments require a booking.
Lan: Healthcare access is difficult for my neighbour because the last bus home leaves before some appointments finish.
An: My sedentary lifestyle is partly linked to commuting, so a lunchtime walk might be more realistic than a morning class.
Lan: I would like a sleep quality discussion that acknowledges shift work instead of assuming everyone has the same timetable.
An: Health literacy would help me ask better questions when an online post makes an impressive claim.
Lan: Social support should feel welcoming, but people must be free to leave a discussion they find uncomfortable.
An: Work-related stress cannot be addressed only by telling staff to relax while their deadlines keep increasing.
Lan: Quality of life means being able to do things that matter to me, so I would welcome a choice of activities.
An: We have several reasonable priorities, but the budget cannot cover every suggestion immediately.
Lan: Let us recommend one change, explain our reasons, and identify evidence that would tell us whether it worked.
>>>
[/CONTENT]
